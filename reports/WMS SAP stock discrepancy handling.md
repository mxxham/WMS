# Explain the book before counting the bin

A plus on one SKU and a minus on another in a daily MB52-vs-WMS comparison is usually not stock loss. Published practice treats it first as a **book-to-book question**: same cut-off moment, same storage-location scope, same stock type, timing of GR/GI postings. Physical recounts come only after those checks. If two differences offset each other on the same day, a mispick or a batch/SKU swap is the leading hypothesis. Practitioner and vendor sources converge on a layered routine. A daily exception list carries fixed reason codes. A weekly review ages the open items. A month-end package signed by both parties proves "SAP book = WMS physical ± explained timing ± requested postings". Count-driven corrections go through blind counts, a recount by a different person, and tiered approval. On the SAP side, the stock owner posts them under its own controls (701/702 via MI07, 309, 311, 344/343, 551/555 or Shell's custom Z55), because a 3PL without SAP access can only *request* a correction. Contracts usually put routine loss inside a shrinkage allowance of about **0.5–1% of inventory value**, absorbed by the owner. The 3PL reimburses losses above it. Best-in-class location accuracy is **≥99.5%** (WERC 2025). Two caveats bound this report. No standards body (WERC, CSCMP, ASCM) publishes a 3PL-to-ERP reconciliation procedure, so most procedural detail comes from vendor and consultant writing. No Shell, Indonesian or oil-industry 3PL standard was found. The CKB warehouse already has much of the machinery (reason codes, four-eyes approval, blind recounts, cut-off reconciliation against MB52/MB51, a SAP book ledger). The main gaps are a tracked correction-request loop to Shell, netting before adjusting, and aging of open differences.

Statements about what the CKB WMS already does come from the repository's migrations (`supabase/migrations/0018`, `0019`, `0021`, `0111`–`0116`), not from external sources.

## A difference is only real after cut-off, scope and timing are ruled out

The recurring advice is one written cut-off and three cadences. One consultant policy: "run an operational comparison daily, review aged exceptions weekly, and obtain a signed package at month-end". It also says the **effective time** of each snapshot must be stored separately from when the export was requested, generated or received. A completed download is not automatically stock at the cut-off ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/netsuite-3pl-inventory-reconciliation-controls)). A month-end sequence from another vendor has five steps. Agree the cut-off with the 3PL **in writing**. Pull and archive both sides once ("do not re-pull mid-cycle"). Build an in-transit ledger of five buckets: inbound not posted, returns not graded, picked-not-shipped, shipped-not-invoiced, transfers. Then require that every variance be "explainable by one of these five buckets or it goes into the adjustment log". The same source notes inbound posting lags of **24–72 hours** ([Uphance](https://uphance.com/blog/monthly-3pl-to-erp-reconciliation-sequence/)). The system-of-record split is conventional: the ERP owns financials and the WMS owns locations and inventory states ([Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/amp/articles/business-blogs/3pl-how-to-manage-inventory-4827/)).

The SAP reports matter because they answer different questions. **MB52** shows current stock per material, storage location and batch, split by stock type, "irrelevant of the period" ([SAP Answers](https://answers.sap.com/questions/7288140/discrepancy-between-mc5-and-mb52.html)). **MB5B** starts from current stock and walks material documents back to a posting date, so it is the report that matches a past cut-off. Posting date can differ from entry date ([SCN](https://scn.sap.com/thread/1254192)), and per-date runs can produce odd results because stock is held per period ([SAP blog](https://blogs.sap.com/2008/06/08/mb5b-offers-negative-results-when-executed-per-posting-date/)). **MB51** lists the material documents that explain movement between two dates. **VL06G** lists outbound deliveries still waiting for goods issue ([se80](https://www.se80.co.uk/sap-tcodes/?name=vl06g)). LX23 and the EWM tools (/SCWM/ERP_STOCKCHECK, Difference Analyzer) compare SAP's own WM against IM. They do not apply when the WMS is external, like CKB's.

**(Inference)** An MB52 export run on the morning of D+1 is "SAP now". It can include Shell postings made after the cut-off. The CKB repository records the consequence: on 28 Oct 2026 the first live comparison set the morning MB52 against WMS stock "at the moment Buat was pressed". Every SKU shipped that day showed as "WMS kurang" (WMS short), which led to migration `0112` and its cut-off at 00:00 WIB. The same logic applies to scope. Shell's book is split between WHS1 and an inbound location. A pallet physically put away but still on SAP's inbound location is a location/timing item, not a gain. Comparisons should therefore run on WHS1 + inbound together first, then separately, and by stock type (WMS Quarantine against SAP blocked/QI, WMS pickable against SAP unrestricted). Migration `0111` already reads the `mb52`, `INBD` and `mb51` sheets separately for this reason.

## Root causes sort by how they clear, and offsetting pairs point to mispicks

Sources agree on two layers. **Book/system causes** clear without touching stock: timing, missing or duplicate postings, mapping/UoM/master data, stock status. **Physical causes** need a count: receiving error, mispick, misplacement, count error, damage, shrink. One consultant scheme assigns each a code and an owner: TIME (integration), MAP (master data), MISS, DUP, PHYS (warehouse), STATE (holds), COST (controller) ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/netsuite-3pl-inventory-reconciliation-controls)). Another insists reason codes be "a fixed list, not free text", and treats the reconciliation as non-diagnostic if **more than 10% of monthly adjustment units land in "unknown"** ([Uphance](https://uphance.com/blog/monthly-3pl-to-erp-reconciliation-sequence/)). Error patterns also locate the cause. UoM errors recurring across SKUs point to configuration. Errors clustered in one zone, shift or task point to people or process ([Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/amp/articles/3pl-business/dealing-with-inventory-discrepancies-causes-and-solutions-4837/)). In an FMCG case, WMS-to-SAP mismatches came from IDoc failures, WMS transactions with no preceding SAP document, and transactions that never triggered an SAP posting. A daily rule-based job proposed the likely cause to the stock controller ([JK Tech](https://jktech.com/success-stories/enabling-business-transformation-by-eliminating-stock-mismatch-reconciliation-increasing-stock-accuracy-and-reducing-financial-risk/)). Indonesian stock-opname (stock count) studies list the same main causes: goods leaving or arriving unrecorded, counting errors, postings after cut-off, and QC-locked stock ([ULBI ePrint](https://eprints.ulbi.ac.id/334/1/BAB%20I%20ok.pdf)). **No source gives a measured frequency ranking.** "Timing is the most common cause" is widely asserted but unproven, so the site's own reason-code history has to supply that ranking.

The investigation order is "book first, physical last". Rule out scope, mapping, timing and completeness. Trace the transaction history. Count only what remains, and never adjust before investigating ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/netsuite-3pl-inventory-reconciliation-controls); [Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/articles/for-business/how-to-reconcile-inventory-variances-4827/)). SAP's own help for its IM/WM comparison says to analyse causes from the list **before** clearing ([SAP Help 4.70](https://help.sap.com/saphelp_470/helpdata/en/c6/f83c484afa11d182b90000e829fbfe/content.htm)). The US Navy procedure requires "preliminary research" before adjustments over $500 a line. It reviews recent transactions, unposted or rejected documents, a survey of adjacent and temporary locations, and catalog data such as unit of issue ([tpub.com](https://www.tpub.com/ask/70.htm)).

**(Inference) Reading today's plus/minus pair.** Three shapes deserve a check before anyone walks to a rack. If the two differences are **equal and opposite on similar SKUs** (same brand, different viscosity or pack), suspect a mispick or a mis-putaway. If either equals **a whole delivery or GR quantity**, suspect a timing or unposted document. If either is a **multiple of UPP** or a pack ratio, suspect UoM. The working order for each SKU follows from this:

1. Confirm the SAP figure is as of the cut-off.
2. Add WHS1 and the inbound location, then split by stock type.
3. Match MB51 601/101 lines from D-3 to D+2 against WMS shipments and receipts by DO number.
4. Check VL06G for deliveries shipped but not yet goods-issued.
5. Test for UoM.
6. Look for the offsetting partner.
7. Only then count every bin of the SKU, including staging and Quarantine, and read its WMS movement history.

## Counts confirm differences: blind first, recount by someone else, net before adjusting

Mature WMSs combine scheduled ABC counts with event-driven counts. SAP EWM's catalogue includes low/zero-stock counts triggered at task confirmation, putaway counts on first putaway into a bin, and ABC cycle counting. SAP's example counts fast movers every 2 months and slow movers every 12 ([SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/basic-customizing-in-sap-s-4hana-ewm/setting-up-the-procedures-for-physical-inventory)). Infor turns a shorted RF pick into a "Request to Count" without duplicates ([Infor](https://docs.infor.com/wms/2025.x/en-us/wmsolh/yxe1612894137851.html)). Oracle WMS 25D can create a count only for the short-picked SKU rather than the whole location ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/readiness/logistics/25d/wms25d/25D-wms-wn-f41381.htm)). The recount pattern is consistent across vendors. EWM automatically creates one recount when a result exceeds the tolerance group, can require a different user, and then stops ([SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/basic-customizing-in-sap-s-4hana-ewm/setting-up-the-procedures-for-physical-inventory)). Oracle recounts up to a configured maximum, then holds the count for an approver, who can approve, recount, reject or leave it pending ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/A60725_05/html/comnls/us/inv/ccadj.htm)). A blind count hides the expected quantity from the counter ([Extensiv](https://extensiv.helpjuice.com/wm-cycle-counting-classic/1619071-blind-count-vs-technical-count-classic)).

Two mechanisms keep counts honest. **Netting**: EWM's Difference Analyzer aggregates differences on the same stock key to show "the balance of the differences", so ERP postings shrink, ideally to zero ([SAP Help EWM 7.0](https://help.sap.com/saphelp_ewm70/helpdata/en/4c/b4e7d24c1f1921e10000000a15822b/content.htm)). Infor holds an item found in the wrong location as "Location Mismatch" rather than a gain ([Infor](https://docs.infor.com/wms/2024.x/en-us/useradminlib_op/sceinmgtug/pms1612893864154.html)). **In-flight work**: movements after a count is created are a known source of false variance ([Infor](https://docs.infor.com/ism/5.x/en-us/ism_onlinehelp/mergedprojects/sm_main/forms/invtopic/variance_report.htm)), so locations are either frozen or the count records whether the location had open work ([Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/articles/business-blogs/wms-cycle-count-4072/)).

Tolerances are usually a percentage of on-hand plus an absolute value limit, sometimes asymmetric. Oracle's worked example allows +5%/−10% quantity and +$200/−$250 value, so it is tighter on gains ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/A60725_05/html/comnls/us/inv/ccapprov.htm)). Study guides cite ABC accuracy bands of ±0.2%/±1%/±5% ([vskills](https://www.vskills.in/certification/tutorial/inventory-accuracy-and-cycle-counting/)). SCDigest reports an older APICS scheme of ±1/3/5% and calls it "dated thinking", because most operations now measure by location ([SCDigest](https://www.scdigest.com/assets/newsviews/09-11-03-1.pdf)). The Navy escalates by value band: causative research at $2,500+, commanding-officer signature over $10,000 or on any sign of theft ([tpub.com](https://www.tpub.com/ask/70.htm)).

**(Inference)** The CKB WMS already implements blind counts, a different-person recount when a count exceeds the bin's class tolerance, a supervisor decision after three counts, and "count every bin of a SKU" (`0019`, `0021`). The missing step is netting. After all bins of the SKU are counted, matched +x/−x on the **same SKU + batch + expiry** should become a Bin To Bin move, which has no SAP impact. Only the residual becomes an adjustment and faces the approval threshold. Netting must never cross batch or expiry, because FEFO depends on them. Cartons are discrete, so percentage tolerances round to zero on small bins. A carton tolerance per class is more meaningful, ideally paired with a value limit, since 20 cartons of a premium synthetic and 20 of a commodity oil are not the same exposure.

## Shell posts, CKB requests: governance and the SAP movement types behind each fix

Adjustment governance has four standard elements. **Segregation of duties**: "counters should not be the same people who post or approve adjustments" ([Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/inventory-adjustment-audit-trail-best-practices-for-compliance-and-internal-control/)). **Mandatory reason codes** from a fixed list. **Tiered approval**, with thresholds that are company-set because SOX prescribes no number ([CPCON](https://cpcongroup.com/insights/physical-inventory-sox-compliance/)). An **immutable log** of user, timestamp and before/after quantities ([Racklify workflow](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-to-process-inventory-adjustments-in-warehouse-operations-workflow-documentation-and-wms-settings/)). One SOP names the common failures: missing evidence, vague reason codes, skipped thresholds, and "posting a journal without reconciling it back to the source discrepancy" ([MangoApps](https://www.mangoapps.com/templates/sop/inventory-adjustment-journal-sop)). Where the 3PL reports electronically (EDI 947), each line's reason code selects the GL account in the owner's ERP. **Positive adjustments are rejected by default** because found stock "can also signal data issues" ([Orderful](https://docs.orderful.com/docs/947-inventory-adjustment-advice)).

In SAP IM, a count difference flows MI01 → MI04 (MI11 recount) → MI07. MI07 posts **701** for a gain and **702** for a loss automatically, and enforces OMJ2 tolerance groups per user ([Guru99](https://www.guru99.com/all-about-physical-inventory.html); [SAP Answers](https://answers.sap.com/questions/3602905/movements-701-and-702.html); [SAP Answers OMJ2](https://answers.sap.com/questions/6765500/omj2.html)). Blocked and QI stock use 707/708 and 703/704. Each standard type has a reversal partner, conventionally the next number (101→102, 601→602), and a wrong posting is reversed, never deleted ([SAP archive](https://archive.sap.com/discussions/thread/497925)). Types starting with 9, X, Y or Z are **customer-defined copies** made in OMJJ ([SAP archive](https://archive.sap.com/discussions/thread/1036798)). Shell's Z31 and Z55 therefore mean whatever Shell configured. Only Shell's SAP team can say.

|---|---|---|
| Timing: shipped D, GI posted D+1 | None. Clears itself | None. Close when MB51 shows the 601 |
| Shipped, GI never posted (in VL06G) | Post GI 601 | None |
| Wrong GR/GI quantity in SAP | Reverse 102/602 and repost ([stechies](https://www.stechies.com/some-imp-movement-types-in-sapmm/)) | None |
| Wrong SKU booked or shipped | 309 material-to-material ([SAP archive](https://archive.sap.com/discussions/thread/497925)) | Correcting movements, if WMS is wrong too |

| Damaged/leaking | 344 to blocked (direction to verify), then 555 or Z55 scrap; 343 to release ([SAP Answers](https://answers.sap.com/questions/7558827/convert-stock-from-unrestricted-use-to-blocked-sto.html); [mohanranga](https://mohanranga.squarespace.com/blogs/movement-types)) | Move to Quarantine with DAMAGED |

Stock-type changes carry no accounting impact; scrapping does ([SAP archive](https://archive.sap.com/discussions/thread/903720)). Because the 3PL cannot post, the documented pattern is a loop. The 3PL sends its snapshot. The owner compares and investigates. Adjustments return as electronic advice or a documented request, and the owner posts them ([SAP Answers](https://answers.sap.com/questions/12408851/3pl-inventory-reconciliation.html); [C3 Solutions](https://www.c3solutions.com/blog-c3/3pl-process-flow/)). Practitioner contract guidance says the agreement should state who may authorize adjustments, which reason codes are acceptable, what evidence is kept (often **12–36 months**), and the submission timelines and audit rights ([Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/liability-insurance-and-contract-tips-for-managing-lost-inventory-with-3pls/)). **No published turnaround SLA for the owner's posting was found**, and nothing establishes that owners must approve purely internal WMS location corrections. **(Inference)** A workable split: location, batch and expiry relabels inside the WMS are CKB-internal. Anything that changes Shell's quantity or value needs a Shell posting, so it needs Shell's agreement. A request is closed only when the next SAP extract shows the quantity matching, not when the email is sent.

## Allowances, KPIs and aging decide who pays and how fast differences die

Contracts typically pair an accuracy SLA with a **shrinkage allowance**. A US vendor gives tiers: 0.1–0.5% for premium providers and 0.5–1% for standard 3PLs. Its sample clause has the client absorb shrinkage up to **0.5% of monthly average inventory value** and treats sustained accuracy under 99% as material breach ([Red Stag](https://redstagfulfillment.com/what-is-average-shrinkage-rate-for-3pl/)). One 3PL publishes 1% of on-hand on a rolling basis ([Founders 3PL](https://founders3pl-com.pages.dev/pricing/shrinkage-policy)). An Ontario drafting guide calls **1–2% of annual value handled** standard ([LawyerInfo.ca](https://lawyerinfo.ca/guides/ontario/business-law-ontario/business-formation-ontario/how-to-draft-a-third-party-logistics-3pl-warehousing-agreement-in-ontario/)). Throughput is a far larger base than on-hand, so which base the contract names matters more than the percentage. Above the allowance the 3PL reimburses, usually at replacement cost. Some contracts split unidentified shrink 50/50. Owner-caused discrepancies (labelling, documentation) and carrier damage are excluded ([Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/what-is-shrinkage-allowance-definition-and-contract-basics/); [Racklify, setting an allowance](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-to-set-a-shrinkage-allowance-for-fulfillment-contracts/)). The same guide advises basing the allowance on at least 12 months of count data and reviewing it quarterly in year one.

Accuracy needs a defined measure. WERC's 2025 best-in-class (top quintile) **inventory count accuracy by location is ≥99.5%**, alongside order-picking accuracy ≥99.68% ([Yale/WERC 2025](https://www.yale.com/globalassets/coms/yale/north-america/documents/white-papers/snack-drawer/YALE-1520_WERC-DC-Metrics-infographic.pdf)). Older WERC figures quoted by vendors put best-in-class near 99.9% and medians around 98.4–99.3%, but secondary summaries conflict ([Honeywell](https://sps.honeywell.com/us/en/support/blog/productivity/how-to-improve-accuracy-in-the-inventory-control-workflow-with-a-dc-walkthrough)). Value-based measures flatter. Miesemer's example: one case off in each of 100 locations holding 100 cases is 99% accurate financially and 0% by location ([SCDigest](https://www.scdigest.com/assets/newsviews/09-11-03-1.pdf)). For date-coded goods, the same article notes the date code can be part of the accuracy test. Retail studies finding about 65% of store records wrong ([DeHoratius & Raman 2008](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:54:y:2008:i:4:p:627-641)) do not describe a closed B2B DC. They show why undetected small losses accumulate into large record errors ([Kang & Gershwin](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07408170590969861)).

Aging is the least standardized area. One source offers illustrative buckets of under 24 h, 24–72 h and over 72 h. Unknown identifiers and incomplete extracts escalate whatever their value. Every open item is recorded "against the next reconciliation" so a restart cannot erase it, and small differences still need an approved disposition, with no automatic write-offs ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/netsuite-3pl-inventory-reconciliation-controls)). Another suggests counting before closing anything over "1% of on-hand or 5 units, whichever is greater", with monthly adjustments trending under **0.5% of units** ([Uphance](https://uphance.com/blog/monthly-3pl-to-erp-reconciliation-sequence/)). **No public source sets a standard closure deadline.** These are customer-specific SOP choices.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "who is right, the WMS or SAP?" to "which bucket does this difference belong in, and who must act?". Because CKB cannot post in SAP, its leverage is evidence quality. It has the bin + batch + expiry ledger and the DO-level pick history. Shell has neither at that grain. That makes CKB the natural "maker" of the reconciliation and Shell the "checker" and poster. The cost of the setup is that nothing closes until Shell acts. The tracked request loop, aging and carry-forward therefore protect CKB as much as they serve Shell: without them, an unposted GI or a stale INBD pallet becomes, by month-end, an unexplained loss charged against CKB's shrink allowance.

The biggest open uncertainties are local. They cannot be researched, only asked: what Z31 and Z55 actually do, whether SAP tracks batches in WHS1, and the contract's allowance base and approval threshold. Until Shell answers, the WMS should record the implied correction in plain terms rather than hard-coding movement types. The first month of reason-coded register data will then show whether timing really dominates at this site, as practitioners claim but no source has measured.
